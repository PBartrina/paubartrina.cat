The pattern in the logo, behind the home page title and in the footer isn't an image. Code draws it every time the site is published, and every publish draws a different one.

## Truchet tiles

The idea is over three hundred years old. In 1704, Sébastien Truchet studied what happens when you fill a surface with a single tile rotated at random. The version I use is the simplest: a square with two quarter-circles in opposite corners. Each tile has only two possible orientations, but once you lay them side by side the arcs join up into paths, waves and shapes nobody drew.

## Where the randomness comes from

True randomness would mean every visitor sees a different pattern, and it changes on reload. I didn't want that: I wanted the mark to be stable, but alive.

So the randomness comes from a **seed**. Any piece of text becomes a number (via an FNV-1a hash), and that number feeds a tiny pseudorandom generator, mulberry32. The same seed always gives exactly the same pattern.

The seed is **the commit the site was deployed from**. While I don't publish anything, everyone sees the same mark. When I ship a change, a new one appears.

## One function

Everything comes from the same function, about forty lines with no library: the menu logo, the home page background, the footer, and the images that show up when you share a link. Each blog post also uses its own name as the seed, so every post has a pattern of its own that never changes.

The home page has a button to regenerate it with a random seed. Try it: none of the ones you'll see were drawn by anyone.
